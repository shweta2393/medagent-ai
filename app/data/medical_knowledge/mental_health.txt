DISEASE: Major Depressive Disorder (Depression)
ICD10: F32.9 - Major depressive disorder, single episode, unspecified; F33.9 - Major depressive disorder, recurrent, unspecified; F32.1 - Major depressive disorder, single episode, moderate
CATEGORY: Psychiatry / Mental Health
SYMPTOMS: Persistent sadness or empty mood, loss of interest or pleasure in activities (anhedonia), significant weight change (>5% in 1 month - loss or gain), sleep disturbance (insomnia or hypersomnia), psychomotor agitation or retardation (observable by others), fatigue or loss of energy, feelings of worthlessness or excessive guilt, difficulty concentrating or making decisions, recurrent thoughts of death or suicidal ideation, irritability, somatic symptoms (headaches, body aches, digestive problems)
RISK_FACTORS: Female sex (2x more common), family history (first-degree relative with MDD - 2-3x risk), previous depressive episodes (recurrence 50-80%), chronic medical illness (diabetes, cardiovascular, cancer), substance abuse, significant life stressors/loss, childhood trauma/adverse childhood experiences (ACEs), social isolation, low socioeconomic status, certain medications (beta-blockers, corticosteroids, interferon)
DIAGNOSTIC_CRITERIA: DSM-5 criteria: Five or more of 9 symptoms present during same 2-week period, with at least one being depressed mood OR anhedonia. Symptoms: depressed mood, anhedonia, weight/appetite change, sleep disturbance, psychomotor change, fatigue, worthlessness/guilt, concentration difficulty, suicidal ideation. Must cause significant distress or functional impairment. Not attributable to substance or medical condition. PHQ-9 score interpretation: 5-9 mild, 10-14 moderate, 15-19 moderately severe, 20-27 severe.
VITAL_SIGNS_INDICATORS: May present with psychomotor retardation (slow speech, movements), flat affect, poor eye contact, neglected appearance, weight changes
LAB_INDICATORS: No specific diagnostic labs; used to rule out medical causes: TSH (hypothyroidism), CBC (anemia), CMP (metabolic), Vitamin D, B12, folate. PHQ-9 screening score.
RECOMMENDED_TESTS: PHQ-9 screening questionnaire (validated, widely used), Thyroid function tests (TSH, free T4 - rule out hypothyroidism), Complete blood count (rule out anemia), Vitamin B12 and folate levels, Vitamin D level (25-OH), Basic metabolic panel, Comprehensive metabolic panel (rule out hepatic/renal causes), Drug/alcohol screening (comorbidity/secondary cause), Columbia Suicide Severity Rating Scale (C-SSRS) if suicidal ideation present
SPECIALIST: Psychiatrist, Psychologist, Primary Care Physician (mild-moderate depression)
URGENCY: moderate (critical if active suicidal ideation with plan, intent, or means - psychiatric emergency requiring immediate safety assessment)
TREATMENT_OVERVIEW: APA Practice Guidelines: Mild: psychotherapy alone (CBT, interpersonal therapy - IPT). Moderate-Severe: antidepressant medication + psychotherapy (combination superior). First-line antidepressants: SSRIs (sertraline, escitalopram, fluoxetine - best tolerability/efficacy balance), SNRIs (venlafaxine, duloxetine), bupropion (useful with fatigue, no sexual side effects, smoking). Second-line: mirtazapine, TCAs, MAOIs. Treatment-resistant: augmentation (lithium, aripiprazole, quetiapine), ECT (most effective for severe/psychotic depression), ketamine/esketamine (rapid-onset for acute suicidality), TMS, VNS. Lifestyle: regular exercise (NNT ~4 for mild depression), sleep hygiene, social engagement, Mediterranean diet.
COMPLICATIONS: Suicide (15% lifetime risk in severe depression, 800,000 deaths globally per year), substance abuse/self-medication, social isolation, cardiovascular disease (1.5-2x risk), obesity/metabolic syndrome, chronic pain, impaired relationships/occupational functioning, treatment-resistant depression (30% of patients)
WHO_GUIDELINE: WHO mhGAP Intervention Guide for mental disorders in non-specialized settings (evidence-based psychosocial interventions + antidepressants); WHO estimates 280 million people affected by depression globally (2021); WHO ranks depression as leading cause of disability worldwide (GBD); WHO Essential Medicines includes fluoxetine, amitriptyline for depression; WHO Special Initiative for Mental Health 2019-2023
CDC_MAPPING: CDC NHIS data - 21 million US adults (8.4%) had at least one major depressive episode (2020); 4.7% moderate-to-severe; highest rates in ages 18-25; disparities by race, income, and geographic region; CDC reports depression contributes to 42,000+ suicides annually in US; US Preventive Services Task Force (USPSTF) recommends screening for depression in all adults
PUBMED_REFERENCES: PMID:28810401 (APA Practice Guideline for Depression 2010, updated), PMID:29345995 (STAR*D study - treatment algorithms), PMID:31268560 (CANMAT/ISBD 2019 Depression Guidelines), PMID:31184986 (Ketamine/esketamine for treatment-resistant depression meta-analysis)
FOLLOW_UP_QUESTIONS: How long have you been feeling this way? Have you lost interest in activities you used to enjoy? Are you having trouble sleeping or sleeping too much? Have you had any thoughts of harming yourself or suicide? Are you using alcohol or drugs to cope? Do you have a family history of depression? Have you tried any treatment before?

DISEASE: Generalized Anxiety Disorder (GAD)
ICD10: F41.1 - Generalized anxiety disorder
CATEGORY: Psychiatry / Mental Health
SYMPTOMS: Excessive worry about multiple things (work, health, family, finances - difficult to control), restlessness or feeling on edge, easily fatigued, difficulty concentrating or mind going blank, irritability, muscle tension (jaw clenching, shoulder tension, headaches), sleep disturbance (difficulty falling/staying asleep), somatic symptoms: headaches, stomach upset/nausea, sweating, trembling, frequent urination, diarrhea, dry mouth, difficulty swallowing
RISK_FACTORS: Female sex (2x more common), family history of anxiety (genetic heritability ~30%), childhood adversity/trauma, chronic stress, other mental health disorders (depression 60% comorbid, panic disorder, PTSD), chronic medical conditions (thyroid disorders, cardiovascular, chronic pain), substance use/withdrawal, personality traits (neuroticism, behavioral inhibition)
DIAGNOSTIC_CRITERIA: DSM-5 criteria: Excessive anxiety and worry (apprehensive expectation) occurring more days than not for >= 6 months, about a number of events or activities. Difficulty controlling the worry. Worry associated with >= 3 of 6 symptoms (adults) or >= 1 (children): restlessness, easily fatigued, difficulty concentrating, irritability, muscle tension, sleep disturbance. Causes clinically significant distress or functional impairment. Not attributable to substance or medical condition. GAD-7 scoring: 5-9 mild, 10-14 moderate, >= 15 severe.
VITAL_SIGNS_INDICATORS: Tachycardia, elevated blood pressure, diaphoresis (sweating), visible muscle tension, hyperventilation, restlessness on exam
LAB_INDICATORS: No specific diagnostic labs; rule-out testing: TSH (hyperthyroidism mimics anxiety), CBC, CMP, ECG (palpitations), Mg2+ (low magnesium causes anxiety), cortisol (Cushing's). GAD-7 screening score.
RECOMMENDED_TESTS: GAD-7 screening questionnaire, Thyroid function tests (TSH, free T4), Complete blood count, Metabolic panel, ECG (if cardiac symptoms/palpitations), Magnesium level, Drug/alcohol screening, Cortisol (if Cushing's suspected), Pheochromocytoma workup (if paroxysmal HTN + anxiety - plasma/urine metanephrines)
SPECIALIST: Psychiatrist, Psychologist (CBT therapist), Primary Care
URGENCY: low to moderate (moderate if significant functional impairment, comorbid depression with suicidality, or substance abuse)
TREATMENT_OVERVIEW: APA/NICE/CANMAT Guidelines: First-line: CBT (gold standard psychotherapy - NNT ~3, 12-20 sessions, comparable to medication), SSRIs (escitalopram, sertraline - first-line pharmacotherapy), SNRIs (venlafaxine XR, duloxetine). Second-line: buspirone (5-HT1A agonist, no dependence), pregabalin (Europe). Adjunctive: hydroxyzine (PRN). Benzodiazepines (alprazolam, clonazepam): short-term only (<4 weeks) due to dependence risk, NOT first-line. Other: mindfulness-based stress reduction (MBSR), regular exercise (30 min/day, 5x/week), sleep hygiene, limiting caffeine/alcohol, relaxation techniques (progressive muscle relaxation, diaphragmatic breathing).
COMPLICATIONS: Depression (major comorbidity - 60%), substance abuse (self-medication with alcohol, benzodiazepines), physical health problems (cardiovascular, GI - IBS), social and occupational impairment, chronic pain/fibromyalgia, insomnia, suicidal ideation (especially with comorbid depression)
WHO_GUIDELINE: WHO mhGAP Intervention Guide includes anxiety disorders in primary care management; WHO estimates 301 million people globally had an anxiety disorder (2019); WHO Essential Medicines includes diazepam and fluoxetine; WHO recommends psychosocial interventions as first-line in mhGAP
CDC_MAPPING: CDC NHIS data - 15.6% of US adults experienced symptoms of anxiety disorder in previous 2 weeks (2023); highest rates in ages 18-29; anxiety disorders are the most common mental health condition in the US; significant increase during and after COVID-19 pandemic
PUBMED_REFERENCES: PMID:31296390 (CANMAT 2019 Anxiety Guideline), PMID:28229078 (CBT for anxiety meta-analysis), PMID:26529706 (APA Practice Guideline for Panic Disorder - applicable framework), PMID:32417154 (Pharmacotherapy for GAD systematic review - Lancet)
FOLLOW_UP_QUESTIONS: What do you worry about most? How long have you been experiencing these symptoms? Does the worry feel out of proportion to the actual situation? How does the anxiety affect your daily life, work, and relationships? Are you currently using any substances (caffeine, alcohol, drugs)? Have you tried any coping strategies or treatments? Do you have thoughts of harming yourself?

DISEASE: Panic Disorder
ICD10: F41.0 - Panic disorder [episodic paroxysmal anxiety]
CATEGORY: Psychiatry / Mental Health
SYMPTOMS: Recurrent unexpected panic attacks with abrupt surge of intense fear/discomfort peaking within minutes: palpitations/pounding heart, sweating, trembling/shaking, shortness of breath/smothering, feelings of choking, chest pain/tightness, nausea/abdominal distress, dizziness/lightheadedness/faintness, chills or hot flashes, paresthesias (numbness/tingling), derealization (feelings of unreality) or depersonalization, fear of losing control or "going crazy", fear of dying. Between attacks: persistent worry about additional attacks, avoidance behaviors, agoraphobia (fear of places where escape is difficult)
RISK_FACTORS: Female sex (2x more common), family history (first-degree relative - 8x risk), major life stress (job loss, divorce, death), childhood physical/sexual abuse, smoking (nicotine), substance use/withdrawal (caffeine, alcohol, cannabis, stimulants), other anxiety disorders or depression, temperament (anxiety sensitivity - fear of anxiety sensations), respiratory conditions
DIAGNOSTIC_CRITERIA: DSM-5 criteria: Recurrent unexpected panic attacks (at least 2) - abrupt surge of intense fear/discomfort reaching peak within minutes with >= 4 of 13 symptoms (palpitations, sweating, trembling, dyspnea, choking, chest pain, nausea, dizziness, chills/heat, paresthesias, derealization/depersonalization, fear of losing control, fear of dying). At least 1 attack followed by >= 1 month of: persistent concern about additional panic attacks OR significant maladaptive behavioral change related to attacks (avoidance). Not attributable to substance or medical condition. PHQ-PD (Panic Disorder module) for screening.
VITAL_SIGNS_INDICATORS: During attack: tachycardia (HR > 120), hyperventilation (RR > 20), elevated blood pressure, diaphoresis, tremor. Between attacks: usually entirely normal physical exam
LAB_INDICATORS: ALL labs typically NORMAL (diagnosis of exclusion for medical causes). Rule-out labs: TSH (thyroid), ECG (arrhythmia), Troponin (MI), D-dimer (PE), glucose (hypoglycemia), calcium (hypercalcemia), pheochromocytoma screen (plasma metanephrines), blood gas (hypocapnia during hyperventilation)
RECOMMENDED_TESTS: ECG (rule out cardiac arrhythmia - mandatory on first presentation), Thyroid function tests (TSH), Complete blood count, Metabolic panel (calcium, glucose), Urine drug screen, Echocardiogram (if cardiac symptoms persist), Holter monitor (if palpitations persist), Pulmonary function tests (if respiratory symptoms predominant), Plasma metanephrines (if paroxysmal hypertension), PHQ-PD screening
SPECIALIST: Psychiatrist, Psychologist (CBT therapist), Emergency Medicine (first presentation - rule out medical cause)
URGENCY: moderate (high on first episode presentation to rule out cardiac/pulmonary/medical emergency; low-moderate for known panic disorder)
TREATMENT_OVERVIEW: APA/NICE/CANMAT Guidelines: First-line: CBT with interoceptive exposure and cognitive restructuring (gold standard - NNT ~2-3, 12-16 sessions, 70-80% response rate). First-line pharmacotherapy: SSRIs (sertraline, paroxetine - FDA approved, fluoxetine, escitalopram), SNRIs (venlafaxine XR). Start LOW and go SLOW (patients are sensitive to activation). Benzodiazepines: short-term rescue (alprazolam, clonazepam) - NOT first-line due to dependence, withdrawal-induced panic, impairs CBT learning. Combination CBT + SSRI often optimal. Breathing retraining, progressive muscle relaxation, mindfulness. Lifestyle: regular exercise, sleep hygiene, limit caffeine and alcohol.
COMPLICATIONS: Agoraphobia (development in 30-50%), major depression (50-70% comorbidity), substance abuse (alcohol, benzodiazepine dependence), social isolation, suicidal ideation (20% attempt rate with comorbid depression), occupational impairment, frequent ED visits (panic mimics MI), reduced quality of life
WHO_GUIDELINE: WHO mhGAP Intervention Guide includes panic disorder under anxiety disorders module; WHO recognizes panic disorder as a significant contributor to disability and emergency healthcare utilization; WHO Essential Medicines includes fluoxetine and diazepam applicable to panic disorder
CDC_MAPPING: CDC mental health surveillance - panic disorder affects ~2-3% of US adults annually (6 million); women twice as likely; panic attacks experienced by 11% of US adults in any year; significant overlap with emergency department utilization for non-cardiac chest pain
PUBMED_REFERENCES: PMID:26529706 (APA Practice Guideline for Panic Disorder), PMID:26805644 (CBT for panic disorder meta-analysis), PMID:27677506 (SSRI vs CBT for panic - comparative effectiveness), PMID:32417154 (Pharmacotherapy for anxiety disorders - Lancet)
FOLLOW_UP_QUESTIONS: Can you describe what happens during an episode? How often do these episodes occur? Do you avoid certain places or situations because of fear of an attack? Does the episode come on suddenly? How long does it typically last? Have you had your heart checked? Do you consume caffeine or use any substances?
