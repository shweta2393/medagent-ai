DISEASE: Influenza (Flu)
ICD10: J11.1 - Influenza due to unidentified influenza virus with other respiratory manifestations; J10.1 - Influenza due to other identified influenza virus with other respiratory manifestations; J09.X1 - Influenza due to identified novel influenza A virus
CATEGORY: Infectious Disease
SYMPTOMS: Sudden onset high fever (102-104°F), body aches/myalgia, headache, fatigue/malaise, dry cough, sore throat, runny or stuffy nose (rhinorrhea), chills, sweating, weakness, loss of appetite, nausea, vomiting (more common in children), diarrhea (more common in children)
RISK_FACTORS: Age under 5 (especially < 2) or over 65, pregnancy, chronic diseases (asthma, COPD, diabetes, heart disease), weakened immune system, obesity (BMI >= 40), healthcare workers, long-term care facility residents, not vaccinated
DIAGNOSTIC_CRITERIA: CDC case definition: clinical symptoms during flu season. Rapid influenza diagnostic test (RIDT) - sensitivity 50-70%, specificity > 90%. RT-PCR (gold standard - sensitivity > 95%). Rapid molecular assays (ID NOW) - sensitivity ~95%. Clinical diagnosis reasonable during peak season with typical presentation.
VITAL_SIGNS_INDICATORS: Fever (> 100.4°F/38°C, often > 102°F/38.9°C), tachycardia, may have low oxygen saturation in severe/complicated cases, tachypnea
LAB_INDICATORS: Rapid influenza test positive, RT-PCR positive for influenza A or B, leukopenia or normal WBC, elevated CRP, relative lymphopenia, elevated LDH (severe)
RECOMMENDED_TESTS: Rapid influenza diagnostic test (RIDT), Influenza RT-PCR (gold standard), Rapid molecular assay (ID NOW), Chest X-ray (if pneumonia suspected), Complete blood count, Basic metabolic panel, Pulse oximetry, Procalcitonin (if secondary bacterial infection suspected)
SPECIALIST: Primary Care, Infectious Disease Specialist (if complicated), Pulmonologist (if pneumonia)
URGENCY: low to moderate (high if respiratory distress, high-risk patient, or complications)
TREATMENT_OVERVIEW: CDC/IDSA Guidelines: Antivirals within 48 hours of symptom onset (oseltamivir/Tamiflu 75mg BID x5 days - most widely used; baloxavir/Xofluza single dose - PA inhibitor; zanamivir inhaled; peramivir IV), rest, fluids, acetaminophen/ibuprofen for fever and pain. Prevention: annual influenza vaccine (IIV or LAIV per CDC ACIP). Hospitalize if: SpO2 < 92%, severe dehydration, altered mental status, high-risk with worsening symptoms.
COMPLICATIONS: Pneumonia (primary viral or secondary bacterial - most common), myocarditis, encephalitis, myositis/rhabdomyolysis, multi-organ failure, Reye's syndrome (if aspirin given to children), exacerbation of chronic conditions, death
WHO_GUIDELINE: WHO Global Influenza Surveillance and Response System (GISRS); WHO recommends seasonal influenza vaccination for healthcare workers, pregnant women, elderly, young children, and chronic disease patients; WHO estimates 290,000-650,000 influenza-related respiratory deaths annually worldwide; WHO Essential Medicines includes oseltamivir
CDC_MAPPING: CDC FluView surveillance - 2022-23 season: estimated 27-54 million illnesses, 12-25 million medical visits, 290,000-670,000 hospitalizations, 19,000-58,000 deaths; CDC ACIP recommends annual vaccination for everyone >= 6 months
PUBMED_REFERENCES: PMID:30566567 (IDSA 2018 Clinical Practice Guideline for Influenza), PMID:30184455 (Baloxavir CAPSTONE-1 Trial), PMID:29485209 (Influenza vaccine effectiveness systematic review)
FOLLOW_UP_QUESTIONS: When did your symptoms start? Have you been around anyone with the flu? Have you received the flu vaccine this year? Do you have any chronic health conditions? Are you experiencing any difficulty breathing? What is your temperature?

DISEASE: COVID-19 (SARS-CoV-2 Infection)
ICD10: U07.1 - COVID-19, virus identified; U07.2 - COVID-19, virus not identified; U09.9 - Post-COVID-19 condition, unspecified
CATEGORY: Infectious Disease / Pulmonology
SYMPTOMS: Fever, dry cough, fatigue, loss of taste or smell (anosmia/ageusia - relatively specific), shortness of breath, body aches/myalgia, headache, sore throat, congestion/rhinorrhea, nausea, diarrhea, skin rash (COVID toes), brain fog, chest pain/tightness, conjunctivitis
RISK_FACTORS: Age over 65, obesity (BMI >= 30), diabetes, cardiovascular disease, chronic lung disease (COPD, moderate-severe asthma), immunocompromised (transplant, chemotherapy, HIV CD4 < 200), chronic kidney disease (eGFR < 30), cancer, pregnancy, smoking, unvaccinated or partially vaccinated
DIAGNOSTIC_CRITERIA: NIH/WHO criteria: Positive SARS-CoV-2 RT-PCR (nasopharyngeal swab - gold standard), positive rapid antigen test with compatible symptoms. Severity classification (NIH): Mild (no dyspnea), Moderate (SpO2 >= 94%, evidence of lower respiratory disease), Severe (SpO2 < 94%, respiratory rate > 30, PaO2/FiO2 < 300), Critical (respiratory failure, shock, MODS). CT chest: bilateral ground-glass opacities with peripheral/lower lobe predominance.
VITAL_SIGNS_INDICATORS: Fever (may be absent in elderly/immunosuppressed), tachypnea (RR > 20), low oxygen saturation (SpO2 < 94% concerning, < 90% severe - "happy hypoxia" phenomenon), tachycardia
LAB_INDICATORS: Lymphopenia (absolute lymphocyte count < 1000 - most consistent finding), elevated CRP (> 100 mg/L in severe), elevated D-dimer (> 1 mcg/mL - thrombotic risk), elevated ferritin (> 500 - hyperinflammation), elevated LDH, elevated IL-6, elevated troponin (cardiac involvement), elevated procalcitonin (suggests bacterial co-infection)
RECOMMENDED_TESTS: SARS-CoV-2 RT-PCR, Rapid antigen test, Chest X-ray (bilateral opacities), CT chest HRCT (ground-glass opacities), Complete blood count with differential (lymphopenia), CRP, D-dimer, Ferritin, LDH, Procalcitonin, Liver function tests, Renal function tests, Pulse oximetry (including ambulation desaturation test), Coagulation studies, IL-6 (if considering tocilizumab), Troponin
SPECIALIST: Infectious Disease Specialist, Pulmonologist (moderate-severe), Critical Care/ICU (severe-critical)
URGENCY: moderate to high (critical if respiratory failure, sepsis, or multi-organ dysfunction)
TREATMENT_OVERVIEW: NIH Treatment Guidelines: Mild (outpatient high-risk): nirmatrelvir/ritonavir (Paxlovid) within 5 days or remdesivir 3-day course. Moderate (hospitalized, no supplemental O2): remdesivir 5 days. Severe (supplemental O2): remdesivir + dexamethasone 6mg x10 days. Severe (high-flow/NIV): add tocilizumab or baricitinib. Critical: mechanical ventilation, prone positioning, ECMO consideration, dexamethasone + tocilizumab/baricitinib. Anticoagulation: prophylactic heparin for all hospitalized. Prevention: updated COVID-19 vaccination.
COMPLICATIONS: ARDS (20-40% of hospitalized), pulmonary embolism (5-15%), cardiac injury (myocarditis), stroke, acute kidney injury, multi-inflammatory syndrome in children (MIS-C), long COVID/PASC (fatigue, brain fog, dyspnea, POTS persisting > 3 months in 10-30%), secondary infections, death
WHO_GUIDELINE: WHO Living guideline on therapeutics and COVID-19 (continuously updated); WHO Solidarity Trial (remdesivir, tocilizumab evidence); WHO REACT and RECOVERY Trials; WHO declared COVID-19 pandemic end as PHEIC in May 2023; WHO recommends updated vaccines for high-risk populations
CDC_MAPPING: CDC COVID Data Tracker - cumulative US deaths > 1.1 million; CDC recommends updated COVID-19 vaccine annually; CDC guidelines for isolation, testing, and treatment; Post-COVID conditions (long COVID) affect estimated 10-30% of infected individuals
PUBMED_REFERENCES: PMID:32678530 (RECOVERY Trial - dexamethasone), PMID:34097856 (RECOVERY Trial - tocilizumab), PMID:35172054 (EPIC-HR Trial - Paxlovid), PMID:33631065 (NIH Treatment Guidelines framework)
FOLLOW_UP_QUESTIONS: When did symptoms start? Have you tested for COVID-19? Have you been vaccinated and boosted? Are you experiencing any difficulty breathing? What is your oxygen saturation? Do you have any high-risk conditions? Have you been in close contact with a confirmed COVID case?

DISEASE: Urinary Tract Infection (UTI)
ICD10: N39.0 - Urinary tract infection, site not specified; N30.00 - Acute cystitis without hematuria; N10 - Acute tubulo-interstitial nephritis (pyelonephritis)
CATEGORY: Infectious Disease / Urology
SYMPTOMS: Burning sensation during urination (dysuria), frequent urination, urgency to urinate, cloudy urine, strong/foul-smelling urine, blood in urine (hematuria), pelvic pain (in women), rectal pain (in men), lower abdominal/suprapubic pain, low-grade fever, incomplete emptying sensation. Upper UTI (pyelonephritis): flank pain, high fever, chills, nausea/vomiting, costovertebral angle tenderness
RISK_FACTORS: Female sex (50% lifetime risk, short urethra), sexual activity, certain contraceptives (diaphragm, spermicides), menopause (estrogen deficiency), urinary tract abnormalities, kidney stones, enlarged prostate (BPH), catheter use (CAUTI), recent urinary procedure, diabetes, immunosuppression, vesicoureteral reflux, incomplete bladder emptying
DIAGNOSTIC_CRITERIA: IDSA/AUA Guidelines: Urinalysis showing pyuria (WBC > 10/hpf), positive leukocyte esterase, positive nitrites (specific for gram-negative bacteria), bacteriuria. Urine culture: >= 100,000 CFU/mL of single organism (symptomatic), >= 1,000 CFU/mL with catheter. Clinical symptoms required (asymptomatic bacteriuria only treated in pregnancy and pre-urologic procedures).
VITAL_SIGNS_INDICATORS: Usually normal for uncomplicated cystitis; pyelonephritis: fever > 101.3°F (38.5°C), tachycardia, CVA tenderness, hypotension (if urosepsis)
LAB_INDICATORS: Positive urinalysis (leukocyte esterase, nitrites, WBCs > 10/hpf, bacteria), positive urine culture (>= 10^5 CFU/mL), elevated WBC and CRP (pyelonephritis), blood cultures positive (20-30% in pyelonephritis), elevated procalcitonin (upper tract infection)
RECOMMENDED_TESTS: Urinalysis with microscopy, Urine culture and sensitivity (always for complicated/recurrent), Complete blood count (if systemic symptoms), Basic metabolic panel, Blood cultures x2 (if pyelonephritis/urosepsis suspected), Renal ultrasound (if recurrent, complicated, male, or no improvement in 72 hours), CT urogram (suspected obstruction/abscess), Pregnancy test (women of childbearing age)
SPECIALIST: Primary Care, Urologist (recurrent >= 3/year or complicated), Nephrologist (renal abscess), Infectious Disease
URGENCY: low to moderate for uncomplicated cystitis (high if pyelonephritis or urosepsis - qSOFA score)
TREATMENT_OVERVIEW: IDSA/AUA Guidelines: Uncomplicated cystitis in women: nitrofurantoin 100mg BID x5 days (first-line), TMP-SMX DS BID x3 days (if local resistance < 20%), fosfomycin 3g single dose. Uncomplicated pyelonephritis: fluoroquinolones (ciprofloxacin 500mg BID x7 days) or TMP-SMX x14 days, or IV ceftriaxone then step-down. Complicated UTI: IV antibiotics (piperacillin-tazobactam, meropenem per culture), imaging to rule out obstruction/abscess. Prevention: adequate hydration, cranberry products (limited evidence), post-coital urination, vaginal estrogen (postmenopausal), methenamine hippurate.
COMPLICATIONS: Recurrent infections (27% recur within 6 months), pyelonephritis, perinephric abscess, renal scarring, urosepsis/septic shock, emphysematous pyelonephritis (diabetics - surgical emergency), pregnancy complications (preterm labor, low birth weight)
WHO_GUIDELINE: WHO AWaRe antibiotic classification includes nitrofurantoin (Access group) and fluoroquinolones (Watch group) for UTI; WHO antimicrobial stewardship guidelines recommend narrowest spectrum antibiotics; WHO estimates UTIs among the most common bacterial infections globally
CDC_MAPPING: CDC NHSN data - CAUTI is one of the most common healthcare-associated infections; CDC estimates 8-10 million outpatient visits for UTIs annually in the US; UTIs account for ~25% of all infections in women; increasing antibiotic resistance (especially E. coli to fluoroquinolones) is a CDC concern
PUBMED_REFERENCES: PMID:29100105 (AUA/CUA/SUFU Recurrent UTI Guideline), PMID:21292654 (IDSA Uncomplicated UTI Guideline 2011), PMID:29370333 (Cranberry for UTI prevention - Cochrane review)
FOLLOW_UP_QUESTIONS: How long have you had symptoms? Is this your first UTI or do you get them frequently? Is there any blood in your urine? Do you have fever, back pain, or nausea? Are you pregnant? Do you have any known urinary tract abnormalities? Do you use a catheter?

DISEASE: Dengue Fever
ICD10: A90 - Dengue fever [classical dengue]; A91 - Dengue hemorrhagic fever
CATEGORY: Infectious Disease / Tropical Medicine
SYMPTOMS: High fever (104°F/40°C - "breakbone fever"), severe headache (especially retro-orbital), joint and muscle pain (arthralgia, myalgia), fatigue, nausea, vomiting, skin rash (maculopapular appearing 2-5 days after fever onset), mild bleeding (nose, gums, petechiae), easy bruising, abdominal pain (especially right upper quadrant), restlessness, saddleback fever pattern
RISK_FACTORS: Living in or traveling to tropical/subtropical endemic regions (Southeast Asia, Pacific Islands, Caribbean, Latin America, Africa), previous dengue infection (risk of severe dengue/DHF with secondary heterotypic infection - antibody-dependent enhancement), lack of mosquito protection (Aedes aegypti/albopictus), rainy season, urban environments, children 5-9 years
DIAGNOSTIC_CRITERIA: WHO 2009 Dengue Classification: Dengue with/without warning signs vs Severe Dengue. NS1 antigen positive (days 1-5, sensitivity ~90%), dengue IgM positive (after day 5, sensitivity ~90%), dengue IgG (4-fold rise = secondary infection), RT-PCR (days 1-5, serotype identification). Warning signs (predictors of severe dengue): persistent vomiting, abdominal pain, mucosal bleeding, lethargy, hepatomegaly > 2cm, rising hematocrit with dropping platelets.
VITAL_SIGNS_INDICATORS: High fever (biphasic/saddleback pattern), tachycardia, hypotension (warning sign for dengue shock syndrome - narrowing pulse pressure < 20 mmHg), petechiae, positive tourniquet test (>= 10 petechiae per 2.5cm square)
LAB_INDICATORS: Leukopenia (WBC < 5,000), thrombocytopenia (platelet count < 100,000 - hallmark), elevated hematocrit (hemoconcentration >= 20% rise = plasma leakage), elevated AST/ALT (hepatitis), hypoalbuminemia, positive dengue NS1/IgM/IgG
RECOMMENDED_TESTS: Dengue NS1 antigen test (days 1-5), Dengue IgM/IgG serology (after day 5), Complete blood count with differential (daily monitoring), Liver function tests (AST, ALT), Coagulation studies (PT, aPTT), Metabolic panel (electrolytes), Dengue RT-PCR (serotype), Chest X-ray/Ultrasound (for pleural effusion/ascites in severe), Hematocrit monitoring every 4-6 hours (severe)
SPECIALIST: Infectious Disease Specialist, Internal Medicine, Pediatrician, ICU (severe dengue)
URGENCY: moderate (critical if warning signs present: severe abdominal pain, persistent vomiting, clinical fluid accumulation, mucosal bleeding, lethargy/restlessness, liver enlargement > 2cm, rising hematocrit with dropping platelets)
TREATMENT_OVERVIEW: WHO Dengue Guidelines: No specific antiviral treatment. Supportive care: adequate oral hydration (ORS), acetaminophen for fever/pain (AVOID NSAIDs and aspirin - bleeding risk), monitoring platelet count and hematocrit serially. Warning signs/severe: IV isotonic crystalloids (NS or RL) - judicious fluid management (6-7 mL/kg/hr reducing to 5, 3, 1.5 as hematocrit improves), blood transfusion if severe bleeding (Hb < 8 with bleeding), platelet transfusion ONLY if active bleeding with platelets < 20,000 (not prophylactically). Dengue vaccine: Dengvaxia (seropositive individuals 9-45 years), TAK-003/Qdenga (newer vaccine).
COMPLICATIONS: Dengue hemorrhagic fever (DHF), dengue shock syndrome (DSS - mortality 1-5% with treatment, 50% without), organ failure (liver, heart), severe bleeding, death, post-dengue fatigue syndrome
WHO_GUIDELINE: WHO Dengue: Guidelines for Diagnosis, Treatment, Prevention, and Control (2009, updated); WHO Global Strategy for Dengue Prevention and Control 2012-2020; WHO estimates 100-400 million dengue infections annually in 100+ endemic countries; WHO SAGE recommendation for Dengvaxia in seropositive individuals only
CDC_MAPPING: CDC Dengue surveillance - dengue is the most common mosquito-borne viral disease globally; CDC reports increasing imported cases in US travelers; CDC Yellow Book provides travel health guidance for dengue-endemic regions; locally acquired cases reported in Florida, Texas, Hawaii
PUBMED_REFERENCES: PMID:25547872 (WHO 2009 Dengue Classification validation), PMID:31876945 (Dengue clinical management review - NEJM), PMID:35143380 (TAK-003 Qdenga vaccine efficacy trial)
FOLLOW_UP_QUESTIONS: Have you traveled to any tropical regions recently? When did the fever start? Do you have any bleeding from gums or nose? Are you experiencing severe abdominal pain? Have you had dengue before? What is your current platelet count? Is the fever showing a biphasic pattern?
