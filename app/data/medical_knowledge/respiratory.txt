DISEASE: Pneumonia
ICD10: J18.9 - Pneumonia, unspecified organism; J13 - Pneumonia due to Streptococcus pneumoniae; J15.9 - Unspecified bacterial pneumonia
CATEGORY: Pulmonology / Respiratory
SYMPTOMS: Cough with phlegm (yellow/green/bloody), fever, chills, shortness of breath, chest pain when breathing or coughing, fatigue, nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, confusion (especially in older adults), sweating, rapid shallow breathing
RISK_FACTORS: Age over 65 or under 2, weakened immune system, chronic lung disease (COPD, asthma), smoking, hospitalization (HAP/VAP), difficulty swallowing, recent viral respiratory infection
DIAGNOSTIC_CRITERIA: Chest X-ray showing infiltrates/consolidation, elevated WBC count, positive sputum culture, clinical presentation with fever + productive cough + abnormal breath sounds (crackles/bronchial breathing). CURB-65 severity score for management decisions.
VITAL_SIGNS_INDICATORS: Fever (> 100.4°F/38°C), tachycardia (HR > 100), tachypnea (respiratory rate > 20), low oxygen saturation (< 95%), hypotension in severe cases (systolic < 90)
LAB_INDICATORS: Elevated WBC (> 11,000), elevated CRP (> 20 mg/L), elevated procalcitonin (> 0.25 ng/mL suggests bacterial), positive blood cultures, elevated lactate (severe sepsis)
RECOMMENDED_TESTS: Chest X-ray, Complete blood count, Blood cultures (x2 sets), Sputum culture and sensitivity, Pulse oximetry, Procalcitonin level, CT chest (if complicated), Legionella and pneumococcal urinary antigens, ABG (if severe)
SPECIALIST: Pulmonologist, Infectious Disease Specialist
URGENCY: high
TREATMENT_OVERVIEW: Antibiotics (community-acquired: amoxicillin, azithromycin, fluoroquinolones per ATS/IDSA guidelines; hospital-acquired: broader spectrum - piperacillin-tazobactam, meropenem), antivirals if viral cause, supportive care (oxygen, fluids, rest)
COMPLICATIONS: Bacteremia, pleural effusion, empyema, lung abscess, respiratory failure, sepsis, ARDS
WHO_GUIDELINE: WHO Revised classification and treatment of pneumonia in children (2014); WHO Pocket Book of Hospital Care for Children - key guidelines for antibiotic selection in resource-limited settings
CDC_MAPPING: CDC Pneumonia key facts - pneumonia accounts for ~1.3 million emergency department visits annually in the US; #1 cause of death in children under 5 worldwide (CDC 2023)
PUBMED_REFERENCES: PMID:31573350 (2019 ATS/IDSA Community-Acquired Pneumonia Guideline), PMID:27418577 (2016 IDSA/ATS HAP/VAP Guideline), PMID:25504019 (Procalcitonin-guided antibiotic therapy)
FOLLOW_UP_QUESTIONS: When did the cough start? What color is the phlegm? Have you had a recent cold or flu? Have you been hospitalized recently? Do you have any chronic lung conditions? Have you received pneumonia or flu vaccines?

DISEASE: Asthma
ICD10: J45.20 - Mild intermittent asthma, uncomplicated; J45.30 - Mild persistent asthma; J45.40 - Moderate persistent asthma; J45.50 - Severe persistent asthma; J45.901 - Unspecified asthma with (acute) exacerbation
CATEGORY: Pulmonology / Respiratory
SYMPTOMS: Wheezing, shortness of breath, chest tightness, coughing (especially at night or early morning), difficulty breathing during exercise, recurrent episodes triggered by allergens/cold air/exercise, intercostal retractions during severe attacks
RISK_FACTORS: Family history of asthma or allergies (atopic triad), childhood respiratory infections (RSV), exposure to allergens (dust mites, mold, pet dander, pollen), occupational irritants, smoking or secondhand smoke, obesity, GERD
DIAGNOSTIC_CRITERIA: Reversible airflow obstruction on spirometry (FEV1 improvement >= 12% and 200 mL after bronchodilator), peak flow variability > 20%, positive methacholine challenge test (PC20 < 8 mg/mL). GINA classification for severity and control.
VITAL_SIGNS_INDICATORS: Tachypnea, wheezing on auscultation, prolonged expiratory phase, use of accessory muscles, low SpO2 during attacks, pulsus paradoxus > 12 mmHg (severe)
LAB_INDICATORS: Elevated blood eosinophils (> 300 cells/μL), elevated total IgE, elevated FeNO (> 25 ppb in adults), positive allergen-specific IgE
RECOMMENDED_TESTS: Spirometry with bronchodilator reversibility, Peak flow monitoring, Allergy testing (skin prick or specific IgE), Chest X-ray, Exhaled nitric oxide (FeNO) test, Complete blood count (eosinophil count), Methacholine challenge test
SPECIALIST: Pulmonologist, Allergist/Immunologist
URGENCY: moderate (high during acute exacerbation with SpO2 < 92%)
TREATMENT_OVERVIEW: Quick-relief: short-acting beta-agonists (albuterol/salbutamol). Long-term control per GINA step therapy: low-dose ICS (step 2), ICS-LABA (step 3-4), high-dose ICS-LABA + LAMA (step 4-5), biologics for severe asthma (omalizumab anti-IgE, dupilumab anti-IL4/13, mepolizumab anti-IL5)
COMPLICATIONS: Status asthmaticus, airway remodeling, respiratory failure, pneumothorax, reduced quality of life, medication side effects
WHO_GUIDELINE: WHO Essential Medicines includes salbutamol and beclometasone for asthma; GINA (Global Initiative for Asthma) 2023 report - stepwise approach to asthma management
CDC_MAPPING: CDC Asthma surveillance data - affects ~25 million Americans (~8% prevalence); accounts for 1.6 million emergency department visits annually; significant disparity in minority populations (CDC NCHS 2023)
PUBMED_REFERENCES: PMID:30724302 (GINA 2019 Strategy Report), PMID:29273423 (Anti-IL5 biologics meta-analysis), PMID:30779714 (Dupilumab LIBERTY ASTHMA Trial)
FOLLOW_UP_QUESTIONS: How often do you experience wheezing or shortness of breath? Do your symptoms worsen at night? What triggers seem to make your symptoms worse? Do you have known allergies? Have you ever used an inhaler? Do you have a family history of asthma or allergies?

DISEASE: Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease (COPD)
ICD10: J44.1 - Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease with (acute) exacerbation; J44.9 - Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, unspecified; J43.9 - Emphysema, unspecified
CATEGORY: Pulmonology / Respiratory
SYMPTOMS: Chronic cough, sputum production (especially morning), shortness of breath (especially during physical activity), wheezing, chest tightness, frequent respiratory infections, fatigue, unintended weight loss, swelling in ankles/feet/legs (cor pulmonale), barrel chest
RISK_FACTORS: Smoking (primary cause - 80-90% of COPD), long-term exposure to lung irritants (air pollution, chemical fumes, dust), alpha-1 antitrypsin deficiency (genetic), age over 40, history of asthma, biomass fuel exposure
DIAGNOSTIC_CRITERIA: Post-bronchodilator FEV1/FVC ratio < 0.70 on spirometry (GOLD criteria). GOLD classification: Stage 1 (FEV1 >= 80%), Stage 2 (50-79%), Stage 3 (30-49%), Stage 4 (< 30%). ABCD assessment tool combining symptoms + exacerbation risk.
VITAL_SIGNS_INDICATORS: Tachypnea, decreased oxygen saturation (< 92%), use of accessory respiratory muscles, barrel chest, prolonged expiration, pursed lip breathing, cyanosis
LAB_INDICATORS: Elevated hemoglobin (polycythemia from chronic hypoxia), elevated bicarbonate (compensated respiratory acidosis on ABG), PaCO2 > 45 mmHg (hypercapnia), PaO2 < 60 mmHg (hypoxemia)
RECOMMENDED_TESTS: Spirometry (pre and post-bronchodilator), Chest X-ray (hyperinflation, flattened diaphragm), CT scan of chest (emphysema quantification), Arterial blood gas (ABG), Alpha-1 antitrypsin level, Complete blood count, Pulse oximetry, 6-minute walk test, Diffusing capacity (DLCO)
SPECIALIST: Pulmonologist
URGENCY: moderate (high during acute exacerbation)
TREATMENT_OVERVIEW: Smoking cessation (most important intervention), bronchodilators per GOLD: SABA/SAMA (Group A), LABA or LAMA (Group B), LABA+LAMA or LABA+ICS (Group E), triple therapy ICS+LABA+LAMA for frequent exacerbators, roflumilast (PDE4 inhibitor), oxygen therapy, pulmonary rehabilitation, lung volume reduction surgery, lung transplant
COMPLICATIONS: Respiratory infections, pulmonary hypertension, right heart failure (cor pulmonale), lung cancer, depression, osteoporosis, respiratory failure
WHO_GUIDELINE: WHO Package of Essential NCD Interventions includes spirometry for COPD diagnosis; WHO Essential Medicines list includes salbutamol, ipratropium, beclometasone; WHO COPD fact sheet estimates 3.23 million deaths globally (2019)
CDC_MAPPING: CDC COPD data - 6th leading cause of death in US; affects ~16 million Americans diagnosed + millions undiagnosed; estimated $49 billion annual cost (CDC NCHS 2023)
PUBMED_REFERENCES: PMID:30177816 (GOLD 2019 Report), PMID:27977475 (FLAME Trial - LABA+LAMA vs LABA+ICS), PMID:29323246 (IMPACT Trial - triple therapy)
FOLLOW_UP_QUESTIONS: Are you a current or former smoker? How many pack-years? Do you have exposure to dust or chemicals at work? How far can you walk before getting short of breath? How many respiratory infections have you had in the past year? Do you use supplemental oxygen?

DISEASE: Tuberculosis (TB)
ICD10: A15.0 - Tuberculosis of lung, confirmed by sputum microscopy; A16.9 - Respiratory tuberculosis unspecified; A19.9 - Miliary tuberculosis, unspecified
CATEGORY: Pulmonology / Infectious Disease
SYMPTOMS: Persistent cough lasting more than 3 weeks (WHO threshold for TB suspect), coughing up blood (hemoptysis), chest pain, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, fever, night sweats, loss of appetite, chills
RISK_FACTORS: Close contact with TB patient, weakened immune system (HIV/AIDS - most important risk factor), travel to endemic areas (South-East Asia, Africa, Western Pacific), living in crowded conditions, healthcare workers, malnutrition, diabetes, substance abuse, silicosis
DIAGNOSTIC_CRITERIA: Positive tuberculin skin test (Mantoux >= 10mm or >= 5mm in high risk), positive IGRA (QuantiFERON-TB Gold, T-SPOT.TB), chest X-ray showing upper lobe infiltrates/cavities, positive sputum AFB smear/culture (gold standard), GeneXpert MTB/RIF positive
VITAL_SIGNS_INDICATORS: Low-grade fever (often afternoon/evening), tachycardia, weight loss, night sweats, cachexia
LAB_INDICATORS: Positive AFB smear, positive MTB culture (Lowenstein-Jensen or MGIT), positive IGRA or TST, elevated ESR (> 30 mm/hr), lymphocytopenia, hyponatremia
RECOMMENDED_TESTS: Tuberculin skin test (TST/Mantoux), Interferon-gamma release assay (IGRA), Chest X-ray, Sputum AFB smear and culture (3 specimens - spot, morning, spot), GeneXpert MTB/RIF (rapid molecular test), CT chest (if needed), HIV test (mandatory co-testing per WHO)
SPECIALIST: Pulmonologist, Infectious Disease Specialist, Public Health
URGENCY: high
TREATMENT_OVERVIEW: Standard regimen per WHO: 2 months intensive phase RIPE (Rifampin + Isoniazid + Pyrazinamide + Ethambutol) followed by 4 months continuation phase (Rifampin + Isoniazid). DOT (Directly Observed Therapy) mandatory. MDR-TB requires bedaquiline-based regimens (BPaL). Latent TB: INH for 9 months or rifapentine+INH weekly x12 weeks (3HP)
COMPLICATIONS: Miliary TB, TB meningitis, pleural effusion, pneumothorax, bronchiectasis, Addison's disease, spinal TB (Pott's disease)
WHO_GUIDELINE: WHO End TB Strategy (target: 90% reduction in TB deaths by 2035); WHO Consolidated Guidelines on TB (2022) - rapid molecular diagnostics as initial test, shorter MDR-TB regimens; WHO estimates 10.6 million new TB cases globally (2022)
CDC_MAPPING: CDC TB surveillance report - 8,300 TB cases reported in US (2022); TB remains leading infectious disease killer globally; CDC recommends targeted testing of high-risk populations
PUBMED_REFERENCES: PMID:34003554 (WHO 2021 Consolidated TB Guidelines), PMID:32649857 (TB-PRACTECAL Trial - shorter MDR-TB regimens), PMID:28673435 (STREAM Stage 1 Trial)
FOLLOW_UP_QUESTIONS: Have you been in contact with anyone diagnosed with tuberculosis? Have you traveled to areas where TB is common? Do you have HIV or take immunosuppressive medications? Have you had a TB test before? Do you live or work in crowded environments?
