DISEASE: Chronic Kidney Disease (CKD)
ICD10: N18.9 - Chronic kidney disease, unspecified; N18.3 - Chronic kidney disease, stage 3; N18.4 - Chronic kidney disease, stage 4; N18.5 - Chronic kidney disease, stage 5; N18.6 - End stage renal disease
CATEGORY: Nephrology
SYMPTOMS: Often asymptomatic in early stages (Stage 1-3a), fatigue, swelling in feet/ankles (peripheral edema), decreased urine output (oliguria - late stage), nausea, vomiting, loss of appetite, muscle cramps, itching (uremic pruritus), shortness of breath (fluid overload/anemia), chest pain (uremic pericarditis), hypertension difficult to control, sleep problems (restless legs), cognitive changes (uremic encephalopathy), metallic taste (dysgeusia), ammonia breath (uremic fetor), hiccups, easy bruising/bleeding (platelet dysfunction)
RISK_FACTORS: Diabetes mellitus (leading cause - 44% of ESRD), hypertension (second leading cause - 28%), cardiovascular disease, obesity, family history of kidney disease, age over 60, African American/Hispanic/Native American ethnicity, recurrent UTIs/pyelonephritis, kidney stones, BPH/obstruction, prolonged NSAID use, smoking, glomerulonephritis, polycystic kidney disease, SLE, multiple myeloma
DIAGNOSTIC_CRITERIA: KDIGO 2012 Guidelines: eGFR < 60 mL/min/1.73m2 OR evidence of kidney damage for >= 3 months. Kidney damage markers: albuminuria (UACR >= 30 mg/g), urinary sediment abnormalities, electrolyte/tubular disorders, structural abnormalities on imaging, history of kidney transplantation. GFR Staging: G1 (>= 90 with damage), G2 (60-89), G3a (45-59), G3b (30-44), G4 (15-29), G5 (< 15 or dialysis). Albuminuria staging: A1 (< 30 - normal), A2 (30-300 - moderately increased), A3 (> 300 - severely increased). CKD-EPI equation for eGFR (race-free 2021 equation).
VITAL_SIGNS_INDICATORS: Hypertension (often resistant), peripheral edema, pallor (anemia), pericardial friction rub (uremic pericarditis), lung crackles (fluid overload), uremic frost (very late - rare with modern dialysis)
LAB_INDICATORS: Elevated serum creatinine, decreased eGFR (CKD-EPI), elevated BUN, proteinuria/albuminuria (UACR > 30 mg/g), elevated potassium (hyperkalemia > 5.0 mEq/L), elevated phosphorus (> 4.5 mg/dL), decreased calcium, metabolic acidosis (low bicarbonate < 22 mEq/L), anemia (low hemoglobin - EPO deficiency), elevated PTH (secondary hyperparathyroidism), elevated cystatin C (alternative GFR marker), low 25-OH vitamin D
RECOMMENDED_TESTS: Serum creatinine and eGFR (CKD-EPI), Urinalysis with microscopy, Urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio (UACR - spot sample), BUN, Electrolytes (K+, Ca2+, PO4, HCO3-), Complete blood count (hemoglobin for anemia), HbA1c (if diabetic), Lipid panel, PTH level (intact), 25-OH Vitamin D, Iron studies (ferritin, TSAT), Renal ultrasound (kidney size, hydronephrosis, cysts), Hepatitis B/C/HIV (pre-dialysis screening), Kidney biopsy (select cases - unexplained CKD, nephrotic-range proteinuria, rapidly progressive GN)
SPECIALIST: Nephrologist (refer at Stage G3b-4 or earlier if rapid decline, significant proteinuria, or suspected glomerular disease)
URGENCY: moderate for stable CKD (high if rapid GFR decline > 5 mL/min/year, acute-on-chronic kidney injury, severe hyperkalemia > 6.5, pulmonary edema, uremic symptoms)
TREATMENT_OVERVIEW: KDIGO 2024 Guidelines: Treat underlying cause (glucose control HbA1c < 7-8% per ADA, BP target < 120 systolic per SPRINT, if UACR > 200), RAS blockade - ACE inhibitors/ARBs (reduce proteinuria, slow progression - first-line), SGLT2 inhibitors (dapagliflozin, empagliflozin - DAPA-CKD, EMPA-KIDNEY trials - now standard of care for CKD with albuminuria), Finerenone (non-steroidal MRA - FIDELIO-DKD, FIGARO-DKD trials - additional CV/renal benefit in DKD), dietary modifications (sodium < 2g/day, protein 0.8g/kg/day in G3-5, potassium/phosphorus restriction in advanced), statin therapy (atorvastatin/rosuvastatin for CKD CVD risk), ESA (epoetin alfa, darbepoetin) for anemia (target Hb 10-11.5 g/dL), phosphate binders (sevelamer, calcium carbonate), active vitamin D (calcitriol), sodium bicarbonate for acidosis, dialysis preparation at G4 (AV fistula creation), hemodialysis or peritoneal dialysis (G5), kidney transplant (best outcomes for ESRD).
COMPLICATIONS: End-stage renal disease (ESRD requiring dialysis/transplant), cardiovascular disease (leading cause of death in CKD - 10-20x risk), anemia (EPO deficiency), mineral bone disease (CKD-MBD - renal osteodystrophy, vascular calcification), hyperkalemia (cardiac arrhythmias), metabolic acidosis, uremia (pericarditis, encephalopathy, neuropathy), fluid overload/pulmonary edema, infections, malnutrition
WHO_GUIDELINE: WHO includes CKD in the NCD (Non-Communicable Disease) framework; WHO ISN collaboration on kidney disease; WHO estimates kidney disease caused 1.4 million deaths in 2019 (43% increase since 2000); WHO advocates for universal health coverage including kidney disease screening and dialysis access; WHO Essential Medicines includes EPO, calcium-based phosphate binders
CDC_MAPPING: CDC CKD Surveillance - 37 million US adults (15%) have CKD, but 9 in 10 don't know it; CKD is 8th leading cause of death in US; $87.2 billion in Medicare costs (2019); CKD disparities: 3.7x higher ESRD incidence in Black adults; CDC Chronic Kidney Disease Initiative promotes awareness and testing
PUBMED_REFERENCES: PMID:34272381 (KDIGO 2024 CKD Guideline), PMID:32970396 (DAPA-CKD Trial - dapagliflozin), PMID:36036150 (EMPA-KIDNEY Trial), PMID:33264825 (FIDELIO-DKD Trial - finerenone), PMID:26551272 (SPRINT Trial BP targets)
FOLLOW_UP_QUESTIONS: Do you have diabetes or high blood pressure? Have you had any kidney problems before? Do you take NSAIDs regularly? Have you noticed changes in urination frequency or color? Do you have swelling in your legs? When was your last kidney function test? Do you have a family history of kidney disease?

DISEASE: Iron Deficiency Anemia
ICD10: D50.9 - Iron deficiency anemia, unspecified; D50.0 - Iron deficiency anemia secondary to blood loss (chronic); D50.8 - Other iron deficiency anemias
CATEGORY: Hematology
SYMPTOMS: Fatigue (most common), weakness, pale skin (pallor), shortness of breath on exertion, dizziness, lightheadedness, cold hands and feet, brittle nails (koilonychia - spoon-shaped nails), headache, fast or irregular heartbeat (palpitations), chest pain (with severe anemia), poor appetite, restless leg syndrome, pica (craving non-food items: ice/pagophagia, dirt/geophagia, starch), sore/swollen/smooth tongue (glossitis, atrophic), angular cheilitis (cracked lip corners), difficulty concentrating, poor exercise tolerance
RISK_FACTORS: Women of childbearing age (menstruation - most common cause in premenopausal women, pregnancy - increased iron demand), poor dietary iron intake (vegetarian/vegan diet without supplementation), frequent blood donation, GI blood loss (most common cause in men and postmenopausal women - peptic ulcer, colorectal cancer, angiodysplasia, hemorrhoids, NSAID gastropathy), malabsorption (celiac disease, H. pylori gastritis, bariatric surgery, IBD), chronic diseases (CKD, heart failure, cancer), infants/toddlers on exclusive cow's milk diet, hookworm infection (global leading cause), endurance athletes
DIAGNOSTIC_CRITERIA: WHO Anemia Definition: Low hemoglobin - < 13 g/dL in men, < 12 g/dL in non-pregnant women, < 11 g/dL in pregnant women. Iron Deficiency Diagnosis: Serum ferritin < 30 ng/mL (most sensitive and specific single test - sensitivity ~92%, specificity ~98%), or ferritin < 100 ng/mL with TSAT < 20% (in the setting of chronic inflammation/CKD). Microcytic hypochromic indices: MCV < 80 fL, MCH < 27 pg, MCHC < 32 g/dL. Iron studies: low serum iron, elevated TIBC (> 360 mcg/dL), low transferrin saturation (< 20%). Peripheral smear: hypochromic microcytic RBCs, target cells, pencil cells.
VITAL_SIGNS_INDICATORS: Tachycardia (compensatory), pallor (conjunctival, palmar crease, nail beds), glossitis (smooth red tongue), angular cheilitis, koilonychia (spoon nails), systolic flow murmur (high-output state)
LAB_INDICATORS: Low hemoglobin, low MCV (< 80 fL - microcytic), low MCH, low MCHC, low serum ferritin (< 30 ng/mL - BEST test), low serum iron, elevated TIBC (> 360 mcg/dL), low transferrin saturation (< 20%), elevated RDW (> 15% - early finding), elevated platelet count (reactive thrombocytosis), low reticulocyte hemoglobin content (CHr < 28 pg)
RECOMMENDED_TESTS: Complete blood count with differential and indices, Peripheral blood smear, Serum ferritin (most useful single test), Serum iron, TIBC/transferrin, Transferrin saturation, Reticulocyte count (low in iron deficiency, should rise after treatment), Reticulocyte hemoglobin content (CHr), Stool occult blood test (guaiac/FIT - screen for GI bleeding), Celiac disease screening (tTG-IgA - young patients, refractory anemia), H. pylori testing, Upper GI endoscopy + Colonoscopy (required in men and postmenopausal women with unexplained IDA to rule out GI malignancy), Vitamin B12 and folate (rule out concomitant deficiency), CRP (to interpret ferritin in inflammatory states)
SPECIALIST: Hematologist (refractory or complex anemia), Gastroenterologist (GI source investigation - mandatory in men/postmenopausal women), OB/GYN (heavy menstrual bleeding)
URGENCY: low to moderate (high if severe anemia with Hb < 7 g/dL, symptomatic with chest pain/dyspnea/tachycardia, active hemorrhage, or suspected GI malignancy)
TREATMENT_OVERVIEW: BSH/AGA Guidelines: Oral iron (first-line unless contraindication): ferrous sulfate 325mg (65mg elemental) daily on alternate days (better absorption than daily per IRON-T study), taken with vitamin C 200mg (enhances absorption 2-3x), avoid with tea/coffee/calcium/PPIs within 2 hours. Expected response: reticulocyte increase in 5-10 days, Hb rise 1-2 g/dL per month. Duration: continue 3 months after Hb normalization to replenish stores (ferritin > 50). IV iron (iron sucrose, ferric carboxymaltose, ferric derisomaltose): if oral iron intolerant, malabsorption (celiac, post-bariatric), CKD/dialysis, severe anemia needing rapid correction, ongoing losses exceeding oral replacement, IBD, pregnancy (2nd/3rd trimester). Blood transfusion: only for hemodynamic instability, severe symptomatic anemia (Hb < 7), or active life-threatening hemorrhage. CRITICAL: identify and treat underlying cause (GI endoscopy for occult blood loss, celiac screening, menstrual management).
COMPLICATIONS: Heart failure (severe chronic anemia - high-output cardiac failure), pregnancy complications (preterm delivery, low birth weight, perinatal mortality, postpartum depression), developmental delays and cognitive impairment in children, impaired immune function, reduced work capacity, pica complications (lead poisoning from geophagia), missed GI malignancy if not investigated
WHO_GUIDELINE: WHO Global Nutrition Target: 50% reduction of anemia in women of reproductive age by 2025; WHO estimates 1.62 billion people affected by anemia globally (24.8% prevalence), with iron deficiency as the most common cause; WHO recommends intermittent iron supplementation for menstruating women in populations with >= 20% anemia prevalence; WHO Essential Medicines includes ferrous salt and folic acid; WHO/UNICEF recommend iron supplementation in pregnancy
CDC_MAPPING: CDC NHANES data - iron deficiency affects ~10 million Americans; iron deficiency anemia affects ~5 million; highest prevalence in toddlers (7%) and women of childbearing age (9-16%); CDC Second Nutrition Report tracks iron status; CDC recommends universal screening for anemia in pregnant women and high-risk infants (at 12 months); significant racial disparities (higher in Black and Hispanic women)
PUBMED_REFERENCES: PMID:33560996 (BSH Iron Deficiency Anemia Guideline 2021), PMID:31394597 (AGA IDA Guideline 2020), PMID:31530905 (Alternate-day iron dosing - IRON-T Study), PMID:29443867 (IV iron therapy meta-analysis)
FOLLOW_UP_QUESTIONS: Have you noticed any changes in your energy level? Do you have heavy menstrual periods? What does your typical diet look like (especially red meat, leafy greens, legumes)? Have you noticed any dark or bloody stools? Do you crave ice or other unusual things (pica)? Have you had any recent blood loss, surgeries, or blood donations? Have you been screened for celiac disease?
