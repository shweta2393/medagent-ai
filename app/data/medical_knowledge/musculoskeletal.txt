DISEASE: Rheumatoid Arthritis
ICD10: M06.9 - Rheumatoid arthritis, unspecified; M05.79 - Rheumatoid arthritis with rheumatoid factor, unspecified site; M05.89 - Other seropositive rheumatoid arthritis
CATEGORY: Rheumatology / Musculoskeletal
SYMPTOMS: Joint pain and swelling (especially small joints of hands - MCP, PIP, and feet - MTP), morning stiffness lasting more than 30-60 minutes, symmetrical joint involvement (bilateral), fatigue, low-grade fever, loss of appetite, rheumatoid nodules (subcutaneous, extensor surfaces), joint deformity (swan neck, boutonniere, ulnar deviation - late), dry eyes and mouth (secondary Sjogren's), numbness/tingling (carpal tunnel)
RISK_FACTORS: Female sex (3x more common), age 40-60, family history (HLA-DR4 association), smoking (strongest environmental risk factor, 2x risk), obesity, environmental exposures (asbestos, silica), periodontitis (Porphyromonas gingivalis), history of certain infections (EBV)
DIAGNOSTIC_CRITERIA: ACR/EULAR 2010 Classification Criteria (score >= 6/10): Joint involvement - 1 large joint (0), 2-10 large (1), 1-3 small (2), 4-10 small (3), > 10 with >=1 small (5). Serology - negative RF and anti-CCP (0), low-positive (2), high-positive (3). Acute phase - normal ESR and CRP (0), abnormal (1). Duration - < 6 weeks (0), >= 6 weeks (1).
VITAL_SIGNS_INDICATORS: Low-grade fever, joint swelling and tenderness (synovitis), decreased range of motion, MCP squeeze test positive
LAB_INDICATORS: Elevated ESR (> 20 mm/hr), elevated CRP (> 10 mg/L), positive rheumatoid factor (RF - sensitivity 60-80%, specificity 85%), positive anti-CCP antibodies (anti-citrullinated peptide - sensitivity 60-70%, specificity 95-98% - most specific), normocytic anemia (chronic disease), thrombocytosis
RECOMMENDED_TESTS: Rheumatoid factor (RF), Anti-CCP antibodies (anti-cyclic citrullinated peptide), ESR, CRP, Complete blood count (anemia assessment), Metabolic panel (baseline), X-rays of hands and feet (baseline erosions), Ultrasound of joints (synovitis, early erosions), MRI (early detection, synovitis), Hepatitis B/C (before biologic therapy), TB screening (before biologics), Liver and kidney function (baseline before MTX)
SPECIALIST: Rheumatologist
URGENCY: moderate (early aggressive treatment critical for preventing joint destruction - "window of opportunity" in first 3-6 months)
TREATMENT_OVERVIEW: ACR/EULAR Guidelines: Treat-to-target strategy (target: remission or low disease activity). First-line: Methotrexate (MTX) 15-25mg/week + folic acid (cornerstone). Inadequate response: add biologic DMARD - TNF inhibitors (adalimumab, etanercept, infliximab), IL-6 inhibitors (tocilizumab, sarilumab), T-cell co-stimulation blocker (abatacept), B-cell depleter (rituximab), JAK inhibitors (tofacitinib, baricitinib, upadacitinib). Bridging: low-dose prednisone (< 7.5mg) for < 3 months. NSAIDs for symptom relief. Physical therapy, occupational therapy.
COMPLICATIONS: Joint destruction and deformity, osteoporosis, cardiovascular disease (1.5-2x risk), interstitial lung disease, vasculitis, Felty's syndrome (RA + splenomegaly + neutropenia), lymphoma (2x risk), cervical spine instability (atlantoaxial subluxation)
WHO_GUIDELINE: WHO Essential Medicines includes methotrexate, sulfasalazine, prednisolone for RA management; WHO ICD-11 classification for inflammatory arthropathies; WHO acknowledges RA as a significant cause of disability globally (GBD study ranks musculoskeletal disorders as leading cause of disability)
CDC_MAPPING: CDC Arthritis statistics - RA affects ~1.3 million US adults (~0.5-1% prevalence); CDC recommends early diagnosis and treatment; RA accounts for significant work disability; CDC funds arthritis self-management education programs
PUBMED_REFERENCES: PMID:20299259 (2010 ACR/EULAR RA Classification Criteria), PMID:27998004 (2016 EULAR RA Management Recommendations), PMID:31408237 (2019 EULAR RA Management Update), PMID:25582294 (Treat-to-target strategy evidence)
FOLLOW_UP_QUESTIONS: Which joints are affected? Is the pain symmetrical (both sides)? How long does morning stiffness last? Do you have any family history of autoimmune diseases? Have you noticed any lumps under the skin? How long have you had these symptoms? Do you smoke?

DISEASE: Osteoarthritis
ICD10: M17.9 - Osteoarthritis of knee, unspecified; M16.9 - Osteoarthritis of hip, unspecified; M19.90 - Unspecified osteoarthritis, unspecified site; M15.0 - Primary generalized osteoarthritis
CATEGORY: Rheumatology / Musculoskeletal / Orthopedics
SYMPTOMS: Joint pain worsening with activity and improving with rest (mechanical pain), joint stiffness (especially after inactivity, lasting < 30 minutes - "gelling"), decreased range of motion, joint swelling (effusion), crepitus (grating/grinding sensation), bone spurs (osteophytes - Heberden's nodes DIP, Bouchard's nodes PIP), joint instability, predominantly affects weight-bearing joints: knees, hips, hands (DIP, first CMC), spine (cervical, lumbar)
RISK_FACTORS: Age over 50 (strongest risk factor - nearly universal by age 80 on X-ray), obesity (knee OA risk 4-5x with BMI > 30), previous joint injury (ACL tear, meniscal injury), repetitive joint stress (occupational - kneeling, heavy lifting), female sex (especially post-menopausal), family history/genetics, joint malalignment (varus/valgus), muscle weakness (quadriceps), metabolic syndrome
DIAGNOSTIC_CRITERIA: ACR Clinical Criteria for knee OA: knee pain + at least 3 of: age > 50, stiffness < 30 minutes, crepitus, bony tenderness, bony enlargement, no palpable warmth. X-ray: Kellgren-Lawrence grading (0-4) showing joint space narrowing, osteophytes, subchondral sclerosis, subchondral cysts. No specific blood test (RF negative, anti-CCP negative, ESR/CRP usually normal or mildly elevated). MRI for uncertain cases.
VITAL_SIGNS_INDICATORS: Normal vital signs, crepitus on joint exam, bony enlargement, limited ROM, no warmth (unlike inflammatory arthritis), joint effusion, antalgic gait
LAB_INDICATORS: Normal inflammatory markers (ESR, CRP typically normal or mildly elevated), negative RF and anti-CCP, normal uric acid (rules out gout), synovial fluid: non-inflammatory (< 2000 WBC/mm3, clear, viscous)
RECOMMENDED_TESTS: X-rays of affected joints (weight-bearing if knees), MRI (if diagnosis uncertain or surgical planning), Joint aspiration/synovial fluid analysis (to rule out gout, pseudogout, septic arthritis), Complete blood count, ESR/CRP (to rule out inflammatory arthritis), RF and anti-CCP (to rule out RA), Uric acid (to rule out gout), Metabolic panel
SPECIALIST: Rheumatologist (diagnosis), Orthopedic Surgeon (surgical management), Sports Medicine
URGENCY: low (moderate if rapid functional decline or severe pain limiting mobility/ADLs)
TREATMENT_OVERVIEW: ACR/OARSI Guidelines: Core treatments (all patients): patient education, weight management (5-10% weight loss if overweight - reduces knee load 20-30%), exercise (low-impact aerobic + strengthening - especially quadriceps), physical therapy. Pharmacologic: topical NSAIDs (first-line for knee/hand), oral NSAIDs (shortest duration, gastroprotection if risk), acetaminophen (limited efficacy evidence), duloxetine, intra-articular corticosteroid injections (3-4x/year maximum), intra-articular hyaluronic acid (viscosupplementation - mixed evidence). Surgical: total joint arthroplasty for end-stage disease (knee/hip replacement - excellent outcomes). Avoid: opioids (ACR conditionally recommends against).
COMPLICATIONS: Chronic pain and disability, reduced mobility/falls, depression/anxiety, sleep disturbance, cardiovascular disease (due to physical inactivity), post-surgical complications (infection, DVT, implant failure)
WHO_GUIDELINE: WHO Global Burden of Disease estimates OA affects 528 million people worldwide (2019), a 113% increase since 1990; WHO includes OA in the musculoskeletal priority conditions; WHO Package of Essential NCD Interventions references joint pain management; WHO Rehabilitation 2030 agenda includes OA rehabilitation
CDC_MAPPING: CDC Arthritis Prevalence - OA is the most common form of arthritis, affecting ~32.5 million US adults; leading cause of disability; CDC estimates $136.8 billion annual healthcare costs for OA; CDC recommends physical activity as key intervention
PUBMED_REFERENCES: PMID:31908163 (2019 ACR/Arthritis Foundation OA Guideline), PMID:31278044 (2019 OARSI Guidelines for Knee, Hip, and Polyarticular OA), PMID:25582291 (Exercise for knee OA - Cochrane review)
FOLLOW_UP_QUESTIONS: Which joints are most painful? Does the pain worsen with activity? How long does stiffness last in the morning? Have you had any previous joint injuries? What is your current weight and activity level? Have you tried any treatments so far? Does the pain affect your sleep or daily activities?

DISEASE: Gout
ICD10: M10.9 - Gout, unspecified; M10.00 - Idiopathic gout, unspecified site; M1A.9 - Chronic gout, unspecified
CATEGORY: Rheumatology / Musculoskeletal
SYMPTOMS: Sudden severe joint pain (classically first MTP joint - podagra in 50% of initial attacks), joint redness, intense swelling and warmth, extreme tenderness (cannot bear touch/weight), limited range of motion, pain often starts at night or early morning, peaks within 12-24 hours, skin peeling around affected joint (desquamation during resolution), tophi (firm subcutaneous nodules in chronic gout - ears, fingers, elbows, Achilles tendon)
RISK_FACTORS: Male sex (4x more common; women risk increases post-menopausal), hyperuricemia (serum urate > 6.8 mg/dL), obesity, high-purine diet (red meat, organ meats, shellfish, anchovies), alcohol (especially beer and spirits, not wine), sugary drinks (fructose/HFCS), medications (thiazide/loop diuretics, low-dose aspirin, cyclosporine), chronic kidney disease (eGFR < 60), hypertension, diabetes, metabolic syndrome, family history, lead exposure (saturnine gout)
DIAGNOSTIC_CRITERIA: ACR/EULAR 2015 Gout Classification Criteria (score >= 8): Entry criterion - at least one episode of peripheral joint/bursa swelling. Clinical: MTP1 involvement, redness, unable to bear touch/pressure, difficulty walking. Lab: serum urate (> 10 = 4 points). Imaging: urate deposition on US (double contour sign) or DECT. GOLD STANDARD: Joint aspiration showing monosodium urate (MSU) crystals - needle-shaped, negatively birefringent under polarized light microscopy.
VITAL_SIGNS_INDICATORS: Low-grade fever during acute attack (up to 101°F), intense joint inflammation with erythema, warmth, and effusion, tophaceous deposits on exam
LAB_INDICATORS: Serum uric acid elevated > 6.8 mg/dL (NOTE: may be NORMAL during acute attack - urate shifts into joint, recheck 2-4 weeks after flare), elevated ESR/CRP, elevated WBC during flare, joint aspiration showing MSU crystals (definitive), 24-hour urine uric acid (overproducer > 800mg/day vs underexcretor)
RECOMMENDED_TESTS: Joint aspiration with polarized light crystal analysis (gold standard), Serum uric acid (baseline and follow-up, not reliable during acute flare), Complete blood count, Basic metabolic panel (renal function critical - eGFR), ESR/CRP, 24-hour urine uric acid and creatinine, X-rays (chronic gout - "rat-bite" erosions with overhanging edges), Dual-energy CT/DECT (tophi and urate deposition - high sensitivity), Musculoskeletal ultrasound (double contour sign)
SPECIALIST: Rheumatologist
URGENCY: moderate (acute flare causes severe pain but is self-limited; urgent if concurrent septic arthritis must be ruled out)
TREATMENT_OVERVIEW: ACR 2020 Guidelines: ACUTE FLARE: Colchicine (0.5mg TID on day 1, then 0.5mg BID - within 36 hours of flare onset), NSAIDs (indomethacin, naproxen - full dose), corticosteroids (prednisone 30-40mg x5 days or IA injection), IL-1 inhibitors (anakinra, canakinumab - refractory). URATE-LOWERING THERAPY (ULT): Indicated if >= 2 flares/year, tophi, or urate nephrolithiasis. Target serum urate < 6 mg/dL. First-line: allopurinol (start 100mg, titrate to target, max 800mg), febuxostat (if allopurinol intolerant/ineffective). HLA-B*5801 testing before allopurinol (required in Southeast Asian, African American). Uricosuric: probenecid, lesinurad. Flare prophylaxis during ULT initiation: colchicine 0.5mg daily for 3-6 months. Lifestyle: reduce purines, alcohol (especially beer), fructose; increase dairy, hydration.
COMPLICATIONS: Recurrent flares (90% within 5 years without ULT), chronic tophaceous gout (joint destruction, skin ulceration), urate nephropathy/nephrolithiasis, chronic kidney disease, metabolic syndrome association (cardiovascular risk), joint destruction requiring surgery
WHO_GUIDELINE: WHO ICD-11 classifies gout under crystal arthropathies; WHO Essential Medicines includes allopurinol and colchicine; WHO notes gout as the most common inflammatory arthritis in men globally; prevalence increasing worldwide associated with dietary and lifestyle changes
CDC_MAPPING: CDC estimates gout affects 9.2 million US adults (~3.9% prevalence, up from 2.7% in 1990s); more common in men and increasing prevalence linked to obesity, hypertension, CKD, and dietary trends; CDC Arthritis Program tracks gout as part of arthritis burden
PUBMED_REFERENCES: PMID:32390306 (2020 ACR Gout Management Guideline), PMID:26352873 (2015 ACR/EULAR Gout Classification Criteria), PMID:28763401 (CARES Trial - febuxostat cardiovascular safety)
FOLLOW_UP_QUESTIONS: Which joint is affected? Did the pain come on suddenly? Is this your first episode or have you had similar attacks before? What is your diet like regarding red meat, shellfish, and alcohol? Have you had your uric acid level checked? Do you take any diuretics? Do you have kidney disease?
